\paragraph{Independent correctness and preservation.}
Table~\ref{tab:correctness-preservation} reports two complementary correctness
protocols. Ten hand-verifiable
cases covered identity serialization, structural edits, metamorphic rename and
split/concatenate inversions, arithmetic, dtype conversion, and sharded
save/reload. All 161 tensor--case comparisons matched the independent PyTorch
oracle exactly, and all 149 tensors outside the declared write-sets remained
byte-identical. Across pinned GPT-2 124M, OLMo 1B, and Pythia 1B checkpoints,
all 518 output tensors matched independently computed expected values,
including all 515 tensors outside the three one-tensor write-sets.
Deliberately corrupted value, dtype, and key-set controls were detected.
Thus, we detected neither an incorrect output tensor nor an unintended change
outside the declared write-set in these 13 cases. This supports exact
tensor-state preservation for the tested transformations; it is not a claim of
universal information preservation.
Custom safetensors header metadata and arbitrary sidecar files were not
preserved, so our claim is limited to tensor state for the enumerated cases.
The oracle follows a separate direct-PyTorch implementation but is
author-written rather than an independent third-party implementation.

\paragraph{Robustness and failure semantics.}
Table~\ref{tab:robustness} reports 19 failure-semantics cases executed through
the public CLI on Linux. All 18
expected non-success outcomes occurred, all 17 applicable diagnostics matched,
and all 19 source inputs remained byte-identical. All 15 failures before
publication withheld a new destination or preserved an existing one. Three
mid-save cases exposed negative behavior: interruption or an injected save
exception left a partial shard directory, and a fault targeting an existing
checkpoint produced a mixed directory. Thus 16/19 cases met the
destination-safety criterion; BrainSurgery does not currently provide atomic
checkpoint publication. The principal result is therefore mixed: validation
and pre-save failures preserved their sources and destinations, whereas
publication-time failures exposed incomplete output. Outputs should be written to a fresh destination and
accepted only after successful exit and independent index/loading validation.

\paragraph{Scaling and systems.}
Table~\ref{tab:checkpoint-scaling} measures a common weight-scaling and
sharded-export operation on ten
checkpoints spanning 70M--12B parameters, four model families, three dtypes,
and single-file and sharded inputs. All 150/150 measured outputs passed the
independent tensor oracle. On the primary within-family Pythia curve, the
direct Python/PyTorch implementation was faster at every size. At 12B it
required 15.50~s and 44.77~GiB peak RSS, compared with 24.59~s and 22.88~GiB
for BrainSurgery in memory and 26.54~s and 28.18~GiB for its arena provider.
At 12B, the in-memory provider therefore reduced peak RSS by 48.9\% relative
to direct Python/PyTorch, but required 1.59$\times$ its wall time. These results
show a time--memory trade-off rather than a universal speed
advantage. This is a single-process CPU/I/O experiment and supplies no GPU,
multi-rank, optimizer-state, or resharding evidence.

\paragraph{Comparison with overlapping tools.}
Table~\ref{tab:competing-tools} compares three genuinely overlapping checkpoint
operations on the same
Linux host, with five measured repetitions per tool and operation. All 30/30
outputs passed a common independent oracle. For regex key rewriting,
BrainSurgery required 5.042~s and 1785.7~MiB peak RSS versus 1.478~s and
1571.9~MiB for \texttt{torch-state-bridge}. For two MergeKit arithmetic cases,
BrainSurgery/competitor median wall-time ratios were 1.083 and 1.036, while
BrainSurgery used 2166.7 versus 2890.4~MiB and 3115.1 versus 3895.3~MiB peak
RSS, respectively---25.0\% and 20.0\% less peak memory. Thus BrainSurgery was
3.41$\times$ slower and used 13.6\% more memory than
\texttt{torch-state-bridge} for R01, while the two MergeKit cases traded
3.6--8.3\% longer runtime for 20.0--25.0\% lower peak RSS. These
operation-matched results are not a general tool
ranking or a usability comparison; Orbax remains adjacent related work rather
than a like-for-like executable baseline.
